# Qatoto — honest product review

_Written 2026-11-03 from the code, not the marketing copy. Companion to `PROJECT_IDEA.md` (the
older research brief) and `PRODUCT.md` (who the product is for). Facts about laws, tariffs and
competitors change fast. Check them again before you put any of them in a pitch deck._

## The short answers

| Question                                                | Answer                                                                                                                                  |
| ------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Does it look like a product-commercialization platform? | **Not yet.** It looks like a video site with a shop attached. The useful parts are hidden.                                              |
| Is the engineering good?                                | **Yes.** Careful, honest and well structured. Quality is not the problem. Focus is.                                                     |
| Can it work as a business as it is today?               | **Unlikely.** It tries to be about five businesses at once, has no real users yet and earns no money.                                   |
| Can it work if narrowed?                                | **Yes, plausibly.** Hardware makers who want to build, improve and export products are underserved.                                     |
| Are there competitors?                                  | **Many, each doing one step.** Nobody does the whole journey well. Two who tried (Quirky, Local Motors) shut down.                      |
| Main selling point?                                     | **"We take a physical product from idea, or from an existing factory, to customers in other countries, with the proof at every step."** |

## Part 1: what you see today

When a new visitor opens Qatoto, the first ten seconds say "YouTube with a shop":

- **The home page is a video feed** with a banner carousel. Nothing says "build a product".
- **The main "Create" button has a video-camera icon.**
- **The side menu uses media and shopping words:** Library, History, Wishlist, Cart, Advertise
  with us.
- **Studio is a copy of YouTube Studio:** playlists, subtitles, copyright, comments, customize,
  earn.
- **The valuable part is buried two clicks deep** under "R&D" and "Store": market research,
  finding a team, funding, the daily build log, factories and quotes.
- **"PROJECT IMMORTAL"** (immortality, teleportation) sits in capitals in the main menu. A serious
  business visitor or investor will lose trust here.

## Part 2: features to add (most important first)

### 1. A step-by-step product journey (the missing backbone)

**What it is:** one workspace per product that walks the user through clear stages:

> Idea → Check demand → Prototype → Make it manufacturable → Find a factory → Get certified →
> Fund → Launch → Sell abroad → Grow

Each stage has a short checklist and links to pages that already exist: market research,
teardowns, talent, quote requests, factories, funding. It also shows a "done / not done" status
that the backend works out, not one the user ticks.

**Two doors in:**

- **"I have an idea"** for beginners. Starts at the beginning.
- **"I already make a product"** for experienced business people and factories. Starts at
  "Find a factory", "Get certified" or "Sell abroad".

**Why:** this turns 186 separate pages into one path a person can follow. Today a beginner
doesn't know where to start.

### 2. A demand check that gives real numbers

A simple "register interest" page for each product that counts real sign-ups. That number is
real proof of demand for the founder, for investors and for factories.

### 3. A cost and profit calculator

Parts cost → shipping and import duty → selling price → profit per unit. Beginners most often
fail on this sum. The data it needs (part costs, freight rates, duty by product code) is already
partly in the system.

### 4. A certification and rules guide

For each product type and country: what approval it needs. For example BIS or WPC in India, CE in
Europe, FCC for US electronics, FDA for food and cosmetics. It is plain information with sources,
never presented as legal advice.

### 5. An "export readiness" checklist (supports the export business in Part 5)

Can this product legally enter the target country? What duty will it pay? What label, warning or
certificate does it need? Who handles returns there? This reuses the existing import-intelligence
pages (by HS product code), freight and rate cards.

### 6. Agreement templates and partner links for equity

Founder agreement, vesting, contributor or revenue-share agreement, NDA. Add links to cap-table
tools (Carta; in India Qapita or EquityList) and to lawyers. **This is how users actually get
their equity or money benefit.** The agreement and the cap table are real documents held by
proper providers. Qatoto shows the way and keeps the record.

### 7. Partners shown clearly

- **Crowdfunding:** Kickstarter, Indiegogo, Crowd Supply; Ketto in India.
- **Prototyping:** JLCPCB, PCBWay, Xometry, Protolabs.
- **Legal and IP:** law firms and patent help.

### 8. Book time with experts

Mentors or specialists a user can book. The meeting happens outside Qatoto.

### 9. One real success story, before anything else

Take one real product, ideally a real company client from Part 5, all the way through: idea or
factory → customer abroad. `PRODUCT.md` already says this is the goal. One real story is worth
more than ten new features.

## Part 3: what to change

1. **Replace the home page.** Show the product journey and the two doors ("I have an idea" / "I
   already make a product"), not a video feed.
2. **Rename and regroup the menu around the journey:** Research, Team, Build, Manufacture, Sell
   abroad, Fund.
3. **Move "Find a co-founder" and "Forum" out of Store** into a "Team" or "Community" area. They
   are not things you buy.
4. **Keep video only where it helps build or sell:** daily build updates and product demo
   videos.
5. **Rewrite the pitch honestly.** Not "we commercialize it for you". Instead: "we help you
   research, build, make and sell abroad, connect you to the right partners, and keep a record of
   every step."
6. **Reframe the Problem Map** as "problems worth building a product for" if you keep it.

## Part 4: what to remove or hide

Hide from the menu first. Delete later once you are sure. That way it is easy to undo.

1. **YouTube-style features:** subtitles, playlists, customize, copyright, comments, earn, watch
   history, library. They cost upkeep, carry legal risk and give the wrong first impression.
2. **Project Immortal.** Turn it into a blog post or one quiet research-programme entry with no
   menu item.
3. **The animated AI mascot.** Fun, but it doesn't fit a serious business tool. Keep a plain
   chat helper, or switch it off by default.
4. **"Advertise with us".** There is no audience to sell yet.
5. **Consumer shopping extras** (wishlist, retail-style cart) until real products are selling.
   The business-to-business side (quote requests, quote comparison, factory directory) is
   stronger. Keep that.
6. **The civic Problem Map** (roads, water), if it isn't reframed as in Part 3. Fixing a pothole
   doesn't lead to a product someone can sell.

## Part 5: how Qatoto can earn money

### The honest starting point

Today Qatoto **holds no money and charges nothing, on purpose**, to avoid legal risk while it is
self-funded. That is safe, but it also means **zero income**. Your three ideas fix that. They make
Qatoto a **service company first, with the platform as its tool**. Many successful platforms
started this way, and every paid job also becomes a real success story (Part 2, item 9).

This is a real change of stance. Selling services means:

- signing contracts
- raising invoices and charging GST
- carrying professional responsibility for the advice

It still does **not** require holding customers' money.

### Idea A: paid research for companies

**What you sell:**

- market research: who wants this product, where, and at what price
- competitor teardowns: buy a rival product legally, take it apart and report on its parts,
  materials and cost
- import and export data by product code
- product improvement ideas

**Who pays:** small and mid-size manufacturers, brands, and founders before they invest.

**Why it fits:** the market-research, teardown and import-intelligence pages already exist. The
research becomes a deliverable, and with the client's permission parts of it become public
content.

**Watch out:**

- Research done for a paying client is **confidential** by default. Never publish it without
  written permission.
- Research doesn't scale on its own: more clients means more people.

**Verdict:** **Good first income.** Fast to start and low risk. Builds credibility.

### Idea B: improving production lines

**What you sell:**

- cutting cost per unit
- reducing defects
- speeding up the line
- redesigning parts so they are cheaper to make

These are classic lean manufacturing and design-for-manufacture work.

**Who pays:** factories and manufacturers, often small and medium ones.

**Why it fits:** the talent, service-engagement and provider pages can run these jobs. The daily
build log can show progress to the client.

**Watch out:**

- You need **real experts who go on site**. This is hands-on work, not software.
- If the advice causes a loss, the client may blame Qatoto. Use clear contracts and consider
  professional liability insurance.
- If Qatoto **sells** an expert's work, it is vouching for them. That is different from today's
  "we only list, we don't vet" stance.

**Verdict:** **High value per client, but slow to scale.** Start with one industry you or your
experts know well.

### Idea C: helping makers sell directly to customers abroad

**What it means:** a manufacturer in India (or anywhere) sells straight to a customer in the US,
UK, Europe or the Gulf, instead of only through local middlemen or bulk exporters.

There are **three ways** to do it. The risk grows with each one:

| Way                                      | What Qatoto does                                                                                                                        | Who is the legal seller                           | Money held by Qatoto      | Risk                                                                                    |
| ---------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1. Export helper (recommended start)** | Sets up the maker's own store on Amazon Global Selling, Etsy or Shopify; listings, photos, compliance papers, shipping partner, pricing | The maker                                         | None                      | Low                                                                                     |
| **2. Marketplace**                       | Runs a Qatoto store abroad where many makers sell; a licensed payment company collects and pays out                                     | The maker, but Qatoto counts as the "marketplace" | Through a payment partner | Medium: US states make marketplaces collect sales tax; disputes and returns land on you |
| **3. Importer / seller of record**       | Qatoto buys stock, imports it, holds it in a warehouse abroad and sells it                                                              | Qatoto                                            | Yes, plus stock           | High: product liability, customs, tax, unsold stock, needs capital                      |

**Facts to plan around (checked 2026-11-03; verify again before relying on them):**

- **The US ended duty-free entry for small parcels.** The old rule let parcels under $800 enter
  with no duty. It was suspended for all countries from 29 August 2025, and in June 2026 customs
  made that suspension indefinite. Every parcel to a US customer now pays duty and needs proper
  customs paperwork. This makes direct-to-US parcels more expensive and more complex.
- **US tariff on most Indian goods** was cut from 50% to **18%** under an interim trade deal in
  February 2026. This can change; check the current rate.
- **Europe is also tightening** on low-value parcels. Check EU VAT (IOSS) and duty rules before
  promising Europe.
- **Product rules apply abroad too:** FCC for electronics, FDA for food and cosmetics, CPSC for
  children's products, CE in Europe. A product that is legal in India may not be legal to sell in
  the US.

**Why it fits:** the store already has freight, rate cards, trade services, import data by product
code and third-party settlement. "Sell abroad" can be the **final stage of the product journey**,
the step most platforms don't help with.

**Verdict:** **The strongest long-term idea, and the clearest selling point.** Start with **Way 1**:
service fee plus a monthly retainer, no money held, low risk. Consider Way 2 only after you have
paying clients and legal advice. Avoid Way 3 until you have capital.

### Platform income later

Once there are real users:

- a monthly subscription per team or per company for the workspace
- listing fees for factories and service providers
- paid featured listings
- referral deals with crowdfunding or export partners (check that each is legal)

### Recommended order

1. **Now:** Idea A (research). Quick income, builds trust.
2. **Next:** Idea C, Way 1 (export helper), sold to the same clients.
3. **Alongside, carefully:** Idea B (production improvement) in one industry.
4. **Later:** platform subscriptions and listing fees, once the journey (Part 2, item 1) is built.

## Part 6: can this work? (honest view)

- **As a pure platform today:** not yet. Too many things at once, no users, no income.
- **As "services first, platform as the tool":** yes, this can work. Services bring money and real
  case studies now. The platform makes the services cheaper to deliver and later sells on its own.
- **Main risk:** spending another year adding features nobody uses. Get one paying client first.
- **History lesson:** Quirky (community-invented products, 2009–2015) and Local Motors
  (co-created vehicles, closed 2022) both had exciting ideas and failed on cost and trying to do
  too much at once.

| Crowdfunding                 | Kickstarter, Indiegogo, Crowd Supply, Wefunder, Republic; Ketto (India)                                         |

| Hardware accelerators        | HAX (SOSV), Hardware Pioneers, Indian incubators                                                                |
| Whole-journey attempts       | Quirky (failed), Local Motors (closed), Wikifactory, Dragon Innovation (check current status)                   |

## Part 8: what makes Qatoto different, and the main selling point

**Different because:**

1. **Physical products first.** Most startup platforms assume software.
2. **Every step is connected.** Research → teardown → cost → factory quote → certification → sell
   abroad. Others stop after one step.
3. **Proof at every step.** Daily build logs and an audit record show real work, not slides.
4. **Honest data.** It never invents a number and always says where a figure came from.
5. **Selling abroad is part of the journey**, not an afterthought.

**Main selling point (one line):**

> **"Qatoto takes your product from idea, or from your factory floor, to customers in other
> countries, with real research, real factories and a record of every step."**
